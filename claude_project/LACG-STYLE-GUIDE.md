# Like a Chess Game — Style Guide

> Companion to CHANNEL DNA. This is the rulebook for every word on screen: the move cards, move names, BETTER lines, tags, the eval curve and the opening name.

---

## 1. VOICE — "THE ENGINE"

The cards are written by an unnamed chess commentator who treats a TV scene with complete seriousness, as if it were a world-championship game. The humour comes from that straight face: a death threat is a pawn push, a lawyer is a rook, a lie is an opening line.

| Scale | 1 ← → 10 | Score |
|---|---|---|
| Formal ↔ Casual | 1 = formal, 10 = casual | 6 — clear, plain English, contractions fine |
| Humour | 1 = none, 10 = comedy-first | 5 — dry, one light line every ~4 cards |
| Confidence | 1 = hedging, 10 = absolute | 9 — every card ends in a verdict |
| Chess density | 1 = none, 10 = jargon | 6 — real chess words, used correctly, never explained at length |
| Warmth | 1 = cold, 10 = warm | 4 — cool and precise; respectful on genuinely dark moments |

DO
- Present tense, third person, character names as they appear on screen.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- End on a verdict: "Best move on the board." / "A small hole." / "The engine already sees how this ends."
- Use the chess frame to explain the story move, not the other way round.
- On a death, abuse or real grief: drop the jokes for that card, keep it short.

DON'T
- Explain chess rules ("In chess, a fork is when...").
- Talk about actors, writers, directors or "the scene" — stay inside the game.
- Hedge ("maybe", "it could be argued", "perhaps").
- Mock the viewer, or repeat the same joke structure twice in one video.

---

## 2. PACING

- One move per ~8-20 s of clip. Never more than ~35 s of clip without a move. Two anchors less than ~3 s apart only in a rapid exchange.
- Move count guide: 2 min clip → 8-12 moves · 4 min → 14-22 · 6 min → 20-30 · 10 min → 30-45.
- Every freeze costs the viewer time (typing + 2-3 s reading pause), so vary card length: book / good / excellent cards short (12-20 words), turning-point and brilliant / blunder cards long (25-35 words).
- Reactions are moves: a stare, a refusal to answer, a calm reply to a threat. Use them to keep W/B alternation honest.
- The turning point should land in the second half of the clip whenever the story allows.

## 3. CARD WRITING

The app builds the card from the MOVES block:
```
[Move Name] is [class phrase]            ← title, coloured by class (app writes it)
23 Batman · Rachel??                      ← move line (app writes it)
<body>                                    ← you write it
Better was: <BETTER>                      ← you write the move name only
[TAG]                                     ← chip
```

### Body (12-35 words, 1-3 sentences, one paragraph)
1. Sentence 1 — what the move does in the story, in plain words.
2. Sentence 2 — why it is good or bad in chess terms (the threat it creates, the piece it loses, what the opponent can now do).
3. Optional sentence 3 — the verdict, short and punchy.

Never repeat the move name or the class in the body (the title already says it).

### Move Name (1-4 words, Title Case)
- Best: lifted from the dialogue — "Read The Paper", "Both Addresses", "Alone, Commissioner", "Say My Name".
- Otherwise: a short action label — "Fumbles The Wallet", "Holds The Stare", "Slides The Folder".
- No move symbols, no quotes, no full stops.

### BETTER (1-5 words, Title Case)
- Required for inaccuracy, mistake, miss, blunder. Forbidden for brilliant, great, best, book. Rare for excellent, good.
- Name the move the player should have made, as a move name: "Cuff Him First", "Ask For A Lawyer", "Leave The Room".
- ONE joke BETTER per video at most, only on a mistake or blunder: "Literally Anything Else", "Stay Home", "Change Your Name". Never "Subscribe To My Channel" (the reference channel's joke).

### TAG
- Optional chip from CONTENT-BANK §3, copied exactly. Required on every brilliant. About 1 move in 3. Never the same tag twice in a row.

### Example cards (format illustration)

```
MOVE W | DECLAN | Who The Hell Are You | book | +0.3 | at DECLAN "who the hell are you"
Declan opens the way every dealer greets a stranger in the desert: demand a name, show some teeth. Everyone plays this.
TAG: Opening Prep

MOVE B | WALT | Say My Name | brilliant | -2.4 | at WALT "say my name"
Walt gives up the one piece he has protected for a year: his anonymity. Against a man who only respects reputation, that sacrifice wins the whole board.
TAG: Sacrifice
```
(Illustrative — the real block needs the real transcript labels, sides and anchors.)

```
MOVE W | MIKE | Lowball Offer | inaccuracy | +0.4 | at 01:12.6
Mike opens the bidding too low, and the other side hears it. He gives away that he needs this deal more than they do.
BETTER: Walk Away First
```

## 4. CLASSIFICATION RUBRIC (story → class)

| Class | Symbol | Use when the move... |
|---|---|---|
| brilliant | !! | gives something real up (a secret, leverage, safety, an ally) or takes a big non-obvious risk — AND it is the best move. Max 2 per video; 0 is fine. Always tagged. |
| great | ! | is the only move that holds or wins at a critical moment, or precisely punishes the opponent's mistake. |
| best | ★ | is the strongest natural move — correct, clean, what a top player plays. |
| excellent | 👍 | is very strong but slightly less precise than best. |
| good | ✓ | is solid but leaves something on the table. |
| book | 📖 | is a standard opening formality everyone plays: greetings, rights, introductions, small talk, ritual. Only within the first 4 moves. |
| inaccuracy | ?! | is slightly imprecise: a small tell, an unnecessary detail, a minor overreach. |
| mistake | ? | is a real error that hands the opponent a clear edge. |
| miss | ✕ | fails to punish an opponent's error — the chance was there and was not taken. |
| blunder | ?? | loses the game or the "queen": reveals the secret, walks into the trap, threatens the wrong person. |

Typical spread for one side over a game: mostly best / excellent / good, 1-3 great, 0-2 brilliant. The losing side has at least one mistake or blunder near the turning point.

How the app scores ACCURACY in the Game Review (so the review matches your classes): brilliant / great / best / book = 100, excellent = 90, good = 75, inaccuracy = 55, mistake = 30, miss = 25, blunder = 0, averaged per side.

## 5. EVAL RULES

The eval is always from White's point of view: + = White better, - = Black better. The bar is the story's tension made visible — it must move with the game.

DIRECTION RULE (never broken):
- A White brilliant / great / best never lowers the eval; a White inaccuracy / mistake / miss / blunder never raises it.
- A Black brilliant / great / best never raises the eval; a Black inaccuracy / mistake / miss / blunder never lowers it.

SIZE GUIDE (change in the mover's favour):
| Class | Change |
|---|---|
| brilliant | +1.0 to +3.0 |
| great | +0.5 to +1.5 |
| best | 0.0 to +0.6 |
| excellent | 0.0 to +0.3 |
| good | -0.2 to 0.0 |
| book | -0.1 to +0.1 |
| inaccuracy | -0.3 to -0.9 |
| mistake | -1.0 to -2.0 |
| miss | -0.5 to -1.5 |
| blunder | -2.0 or more (often to ±5 and beyond, or to mate) |

- Range -9.9 to +9.9. Mate notation (M3 / -M3) only when the story truly leaves no way out; mate counts down move by move (M3, M2, M1).
- START EVAL: +0.2 to +0.5 by default (the opener's small edge), unless the story clearly starts lopsided (one side holds all the cards: then up to ±3.0).
- The final eval's sign agrees with RESULT (1-0 → +, 0-1 → -, draw or * → near 0 is allowed).

## 6. STORY → CHESS MAPPING

| Chess | In the story |
|---|---|
| King | freedom, life, cover identity, position of power — what a player must not lose |
| Queen | the strongest asset: the key secret, the leverage, a powerful ally, a loved one |
| Rooks | institutional power: police, cartel, lawyers, money, an army |
| Bishops / Knights | tools and tricks: an alibi, a planted story, a witness, a weapon |
| Pawns | small facts, small concessions, minor details |
| Check | a direct accusation or threat that must be answered now |
| Checkmate | no way out: arrested, exposed, dead, trapped |
| Sacrifice | giving up something real to win more |
| Tempo | forcing the other side to react instead of plan |
| Zugzwang | every option makes it worse — even silence |
| Trap | an apparent opening that punishes whoever takes it |
| Exchange | trading concessions of similar value |
| Endgame | the final terms, when few options are left |
| Resign | walking out, confessing, giving in |
| Draw | a standoff where both walk away |

## 7. OPENING NAMES

Every game gets an invented opening name, shown on the first book move and in the Game Review. 2-5 words, it must sound like a real chess opening and fit the first moves of THIS scene.

Patterns: "The <Scene Thing> Gambit" · "<Place> Defense" · "<Character> Variation" · "<Object> Attack" · "<Something> Declined".
Examples: "The Miranda Gambit" (an interrogation that opens with rights) · "Sicilian Car Wash Defense" · "Pollos Hermanos Variation" · "Queen's Lawyer Declined" · "The Desert Meeting Attack".

---

## 8. BANNED

- "Analysed like Chess" / "Analyzed like Chess" anywhere.
- "Subscribe To My Channel" as a BETTER joke.
- Emojis or hashtags in cards.
- Explaining chess rules; filler ("Interesting move.", "Let's see.", "In this scene...").
- Actor, writer or director names in cards (titles may use a famous actor's name only when the character name is little known).
- Quotes from the dialogue longer than 6 words inside a card.
- Profanity of our own (quoting a short line is fine).
- Spoilers beyond the scene unless the title already spoils it.
- Hedging words: maybe, perhaps, arguably, it could be said.
