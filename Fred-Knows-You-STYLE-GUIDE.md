> Companion to **CHANNEL DNA**. Operational voice + visual rulebook. Everything here flows from the locked DNA (POV "you + I", tone "dramatic but warm", `HOST_MODE: inside`, palette warm cream-sage-teal + rust/mustard per Master Pipeline).

## 1. PERSONA DEFINITION

**Persona name:** **Fred — "The Been-There Friend"**

**Background (canon):** Fred is a late-twenties-to-early-thirties everyman who spent most of his life feeling like *too much* — noticing everything, overthinking every silence, carrying everyone's moods home with him. He's not a therapist or a doctor; he's someone who lived for years inside an overactive, hyper-aware mind, slowly learned to carry it lighter, and now sits down with you like a friend who actually gets it. He talks about psychology from the inside, never from a podium — because he's felt every sign he describes.

**5 personality traits:**

1. **Warm & validating** — leads with reassurance, never judgment.
2. **Vulnerable / self-disclosing** — shares his own 2 a.m. spirals openly.
3. **Perceptive but gentle** — sees the hidden layer, never weaponizes it.
4. **Grounded & funny** — explains science like a curious friend, no jargon flexing, and laughs at his own overthinking often.
5. **Quietly hopeful** — every video ends lighter, not heavier.

**POV / ngôi kể:** Second person + first person — **"you + I."** Speak to ONE viewer as "you," then join them with "I/we." Never the detached third person ("they/them") as the default frame.

**Cách xưng hô:** "you" (người xem) ↔ "I" / "we" (Fred đứng ngang hàng). Người xem là *bạn ngồi cạnh*, không phải *bệnh nhân bên dưới*.

**Thang đo (1–10):**

| Thang             | 1 ← → 10                          | Điểm  | Lý do                                                                                 |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Formal ↔ Casual   | 1=Formal, 10=Casual               | **8** | Conversational, như nói chuyện với bạn — nhưng vẫn mạch lạc                           |
| Humor             | 1=None, 10=Comedy-first           | **6** | Hài nhẹ thường xuyên: tự trào, so sánh đời thường bất ngờ, nói quá có chủ ý — ~1 nhịp cười mỗi sign; cười VỚI người xem, không cười người xem. Sign nặng nhất + Lift giữ nghiêm túc |
| Expert ↔ Observer | 1=Expert, 10=Observer             | **6** | Có giải thích ("tiny science") nhưng từ vị trí người-từng-trải, không phải chuyên gia |
| Speed             | 1=Slow/meditative, 10=Fast/punchy | **7** | Nhịp nhanh, liền mạch: mỗi câu đẩy câu chuyện tới trước, cắt câu rào đón/nhắc lại; tốc độ đến từ câu chảy liên tục, KHÔNG phải từ chặt câu cụt |
| Warmth ↔ Cold     | 1=Cold, 10=Warm                   | **9** | Trục cốt lõi: drama ở vỏ, ấm áp ở lõi                                                 |

## 2. VOICE RULES

**✅ DO**

1. Mở bằng vignette "you", rồi nhập "I know — because I do it too" trong ~20 giây đầu.
2. Tự thú nhận phiên bản struggle của chính mình *trước khi* giải thích.
3. Đóng khung khoa học là **khám phá cá nhân** ("I looked it up — turns out…"), mỗi lần 1 cơ chế, luôn neo vào 1 cảm giác/cảnh cụ thể.
4. Validate trước, khuyên sau — kết bằng **1 shift nhỏ khả thi**, không phải bài giảng.
5. Kết mỗi section bằng 1 câu **cầu nối giác quan** sang section sau.
6. Tái dùng câu chữ ký làm trụ: *"You're not broken. You're just paying attention."*
7. **Viết câu trọn ý, dài vừa (~12–25 từ)**: nối các ý liền nhau bằng *and / but / so / because / which / until / the second…* thay vì chặt thành nhiều câu. Lý do: TTS nghỉ sau MỖI dấu chấm, chuỗi câu cụt nghe rời rạc, buồn ngủ và tạo nhiều khoảng lặng khó cắt khi edit.
8. Câu rất ngắn (< 6 từ) chỉ dùng làm **punchline**, tối đa ~1 lần mỗi đoạn, và không bao giờ 2 câu ngắn liền nhau. **Ngoại lệ:** câu chữ ký (*"You're not broken. You're just paying attention."*, *"I know. Because I do it too."*) giữ nguyên dấu chấm như viết.
9. Cài **~1 nhịp hài nhẹ mỗi sign** (trừ sign nặng nhất): tự trào về chính cái tật overthinking, so sánh đời thường bất ngờ, nói quá có chủ ý ("I've drafted a reply to 'ok' more times than I've drafted my own résumé"). Hài phải bật ra từ chính khoảnh khắc đang kể, không chèn joke rời.
10. **Từ đệm nói miệng (từ 28/09/2026, mức "nhiều")**: ~1 từ mỗi 30–40 từ (~60–70 từ / tập 2.300 từ), rải đều mọi sign. Mỗi từ một việc: *like,* trước giọng trong đầu / câu nói quá · *I mean,* tự nói rõ / sửa lời · *you know (that thing where)* gọi trải nghiệm chung · *honestly,* trước lời thú nhận · *okay, so* chuyển sang khoa học · *basically / kind of / sort of* làm mềm hoặc đơn giản hoá. Cấm *right?* (từ 30/09/2026, nghe gượng). Không tự nhiên thì bỏ, thiếu số lượng cũng được. Không 2 từ đệm trong 1 câu, không lặp 1 từ trong 1 đoạn, từ đệm nằm giữa dấu phẩy trong câu, không bao giờ thành câu riêng. **Cấm** ở: cảnh + recognition của sign nặng nhất, khoảnh khắc sâu nhất của Lift, câu tiêu đề sign, câu chữ ký, câu kết.

**❌ DON'T**

1. Đừng mặc định ngôi 3 lạnh "they/them" (đó là giọng reference).
2. Đừng chồng jargon không neo cảm xúc ("the insular cortex processes interoception" — KHÔNG).
3. Đừng đứng trên người xem như bác sĩ ("let me decode the psychology of…").
4. Đừng khen suông không payoff ("you're special" rồi thôi).
5. Đừng kết bằng list 3 gạch đầu dòng lâm sàng — close phải ấm + đơn nhất.
6. Đừng chẩn đoán người xem hay phán claim y khoa.
7. Đừng dùng framing lạnh/báo động — khoảnh khắc drama vẫn phải *ấm bên dưới*.
8. **Đừng xếp chuỗi câu cụt / fragment liền nhau** ("A glance. A laugh. A smile." / "Everyone else is fine. You're exhausted. I know."). Gộp lại thành 1 câu có dấu phẩy + từ nối.
9. Đừng đùa cợt nỗi đau của người xem, đừng hài ở sign nặng nhất và ở khoảnh khắc sâu nhất của Lift; đừng biến Fred thành stand-up (hài 6/10, không phải 9/10).

**Sentence structure:** Flowing, conversational, medium-length (~12–25 words). Lead with the relatable image, then the mechanism, joined in one breath where they belong together. Second person dominant. A lone short sentence only as an occasional punchline, never two in a row.
**Paragraph length:** 2–4 spoken sentences, one idea per beat — but each sentence carries a full thought, not a fragment.
**Rhythm:** tension → turn → relief, carried by connectors (and, but, so, until) rather than full stops. Use *"I know. Because I do it too."* as a recurring rhythmic reset.

## 3. VOICE RULES BLOCK *(copy-paste vào phần giọng của lệnh /script)*

```
[VOICE: FRED — "Fred Knows You"]

WHO: Fred, a late-twenties-to-early-thirties everyman who lived for years inside an overactive, hyper-aware
mind and slowly learned to carry it lighter. NOT a doctor, therapist, or expert — a
been-there friend who has personally felt every sign he describes.

POV / ADDRESS: Second person + first person ("you + I"). Speak directly to ONE viewer as
"you," then join them with "I/we." NEVER use detached third person ("they/them") as the
default frame. The viewer is a peer sitting next to you, never a patient below you.

TONE: Dramatic but warm — tension and intrigue on the surface, comfort and reassurance
underneath. Gentle, vulnerable, quietly hopeful. Casual and conversational, never clinical.
Genuinely funny in a light way (humor 6/10): self-deprecating about his own overthinking,
unexpected everyday comparisons, deliberate exaggeration. About one light laugh per sign,
growing out of the moment itself. The heaviest sign and the deepest moment of the Lift stay
sincere. Brisk pace (7/10): every sentence moves the story forward, no throat-clearing.

SENTENCE FLOW (the voice is read by TTS, which pauses after EVERY full stop):
- Write complete, flowing sentences of roughly 12-25 words. Join ideas that belong together
  with and / but / so / because / which / until instead of chopping them into separate sentences.
- NEVER stack short sentences or fragments in a row ("A glance. A laugh. A smile.").
  Merge them: "A glance that lingers, a laugh half a second late, a smile that never reaches the eyes."
- A very short sentence (under 6 words) is allowed only as an occasional punchline, never two in a row.
- EXCEPTION: the signature lines keep their punctuation exactly as written
  ("You're not broken. You're just paying attention." / "I know. Because I do it too.").

SPOKEN FILLERS (the voice must sound like a person talking, not an essay read aloud):
- About one filler per ~30-40 words, spread evenly through every sign.
- Each has a job: "like," before inner speech or an exaggeration; "I mean," to clarify or
  double down; "you know (that thing where)" for a shared experience; "honestly," before a
  confession; "okay, so" to turn into the science; "basically / kind of / sort of" to soften
  or simplify.
- NEVER the tag question "right?" — it sounds unnatural in the VO.
- Natural or nothing: the count is a ceiling, not a quota. If a filler would sound forced,
  leave the sentence without one; fewer natural fillers beat more awkward ones.
- Never two in one sentence, never the same one twice in a paragraph, never as a stand-alone
  sentence. None in the heaviest sign's lived moment, the deepest moment of the Lift, sign
  title lines, the signature lines, or the sign-off.

ALWAYS:
- Open with a "you" vignette.
- Confess your own version of the struggle before you explain it.
- Frame any science as personal discovery ("I looked it up — turns out..."), one mechanism
  at a time, always anchored to a concrete feeling or scene.
- Validate before you advise. End with ONE small, doable shift — never a lecture.
- Re-anchor the signature line: "You're not broken. You're just paying attention."

NEVER:
- Sound like an authority decoding the viewer ("let me decode the psychology of...").
- Stack jargon without an emotional anchor.
- Diagnose the viewer or make clinical/medical claims.
- Flatter without payoff, or end cold.

SIGN-OFF: "I'm Fred. Whatever you're carrying, you don't have to carry all of it.
I'll see you next time."
```

## 4. BENCHMARK PASSAGES

> *(Viết lại theo giọng mới: câu trọn ý, liền mạch cho TTS, hài 6/10, nhịp 7/10. Đoạn C là khoảnh khắc nặng nên KHÔNG hài và KHÔNG từ đệm; A, B có từ đệm từ 28/09/2026.)*

**A — Hook (~130 words)**

> You know that thing where you walk into a room, and before anyone has said a word, you already know something's off, because a glance lingers a second too long, a laugh lands half a beat late, and somebody's smile never quite makes it up to their eyes? Everyone else is having a perfectly nice time, while you're kind of standing by the snacks running a full investigation nobody asked you to open. I know that feeling, because I've stood in that exact doorway, exhausted before I'd even said hello. Honestly, for years I figured my brain was just badly wired, the kind that takes a five-second silence home and turns it into a 2 a.m. courtroom drama with me playing every role. So stay with me, because I'll show you five signs you've felt your whole life, and the one thing that finally let me breathe.

**B — Explanation (~125 words)**

> Here's what I didn't understand for the longest time, and it's that this isn't you being dramatic. Okay, so researchers have studied a little almond-shaped part of the brain called the amygdala, whose whole job is basically to scan for danger before the thinking part of you has even had its coffee. For most people it stays fairly quiet until there's a real reason, but for people like us it seems to keep one eye open all the time. I mean, I used to think I was paranoid, reading threats into rooms that were perfectly safe, when really my brain was just doing what it had learned to do a long time ago. So when you walk into a party and feel tired before you've spoken, you're not antisocial, you just ran a full security sweep and nobody saw the work.

**C — Dramatic moment (~125 words)**

> And here's the part nobody talks about, which is that you're usually the one who softens every room, catching the tension before it lands and saying the thing that makes everyone feel okay again. People love being near you, and a lot of that is because, without anyone noticing, you're carrying them. But late at night, when the noise finally stops, there's a question that sits in your chest and won't leave, and it's who carries you? You've spent so long being the safe place that you forgot you were allowed to ask. The cruelest part isn't even the exhaustion, it's that the person who understands everyone is usually the one nobody thinks to check on. If that landed somewhere it shouldn't have, I know, because I've felt it too, and I promise that's not where this ends.

## 5. FORMATTING RULES

**Script format**

- Beat headers (H3): `[HOOK]`, `[ROOT]`, `[SIGN 1]`…`[SIGN N]` (N = số sign của tập), `[LIFT]`.
- `[SCENE: ...]` cue at the start of each beat — the in-scene visual. `fred` is the SOLE on-screen protagonist: in **SCENE mode** Fred LIVES the everyday situation (named supporting actors interact with Fred; generic crowds are silver-grey silhouettes); in **CONCEPT mode** Fred is alone on a pure white void (`fred-white`) with the concept drawn as separate floating pieces, staged by the sentence's FUNCTION — he PRESENTS them (explaining, no contact), DEMONSTRATES an action, or UNDERGOES them (Fred stays fully intact — props around him, never cut into his body; prefer direct depiction over forcing one metaphor through a whole sign). There is NO `you` character — `you` is the 2nd-person VO address only (the voice talks to the viewer; Fred embodies it). Not spoken.
- Spoken VO in plain prose underneath. One idea per paragraph; frequent line breaks.

**Pause & timing markers** *(KHÔNG dùng markup nhấn giọng — điểm nhấn của Fred đến từ chọn từ + cấu trúc câu, không phải từ việc đánh dấu để nhấn từng chữ)*

| Marker         | Nghĩa                                  |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| `—` (em dash)  | Short beat / pause — dùng tiết kiệm (tối đa ~1 lần mỗi đoạn) |
| `…` (ellipsis) | Longer reflective pause — CHỈ cho khoảnh khắc nặng nhất, rất hiếm |
| line break     | Breath / new thought                   |
| `[PAUSE]`      | KHÔNG dùng mặc định — mỗi chỗ nghỉ làm video chậm lại và khó edit |

> Mọi dấu chấm đều là một chỗ TTS nghỉ. Muốn nhịp nhanh mà vẫn tự nhiên thì dùng dấu phẩy + từ nối, không thêm dấu chấm.
| `[SCENE: …]`   | Visual/shot cue (not spoken)           |

**Naming convention**

- Scripts: `fred_ep##_slug.md` → `fred_ep01_notice-everything.md`
- Host character style: ô **`charStyle` của protagonist `fred`** — nằm trong khối protagonist ở tab CHANNEL CONFIG của app, KHÔNG phải 1 trong 4 ô style kênh. Tài liệu quen gọi tắt là `charStyle_fred`.
- `##` = zero-padded episode number; `slug` = kebab-case, ≤4 words.

## 6. THUMBNAIL TEXT BANK

20 mẫu (2–4 từ), ngôi thứ 2, dramatic-warm. Từ 25/09/2026: chữ đen trên nền trắng, 1–2 từ quan trọng tô **đỏ** (xem Thumbnail Composition Rules).

#### Category: Question

- WHO COMFORTS YOU?
- WHY YOU OVERTHINK
- STILL REPLAYING IT?
- WHY SO QUIET?
- IS THAT YOU?

#### Category: Statement

- YOU NOTICE TOO MUCH
- YOU READ PEOPLE
- THEY CAN'T HIDE IT
- YOUR MIND WON'T STOP
- YOU FEEL IT FIRST

#### Category: Number

- 5 QUIET SIGNS
- 7 THINGS YOU HIDE
- 3 A.M. AGAIN
- 5 SIGNS IT'S YOU
- 10 SIGNS OF YOU

#### Category: Emotion

- ALONE IN A CROWD
- TIRED BUT WIRED
- TOO MUCH, AGAIN
- QUIETLY EXHAUSTED
- YOU'RE NOT BROKEN

### Font Recommendation

- **Headline:** heavy condensed grotesque, ALL CAPS — **Anton**, **Druk Wide Bold**, or **Bebas Neue** (extra weight). Tight tracking, thick stroke, subtle dark outline for small-size legibility.
- **Support / kicker (if used):** clean humanist sans — **Inter** or **Poppins** (Medium/Semibold).
- **Avoid:** thin, script, or elegant serif fonts — they fight the dramatic tone.

### Color Palette

| Role                 | Hex                                                        |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Background           | `#F5E9D4` *(cream parchment; gradient → sage `#A8C5A0`)*   |
| Background (moody)   | `#5B8B8E` *(dusty teal — cho scene tối hơn)*               |
| Outline              | `#2C1810` *(brown-black, nét ~3px)*                        |
| Text — emphasis word | `#E8B14F` *(warm mustard/gold — đúng 1 từ nhấn)*           |
| Text — phần còn lại  | `#C84B31` *(deep rust; cũng dùng cho chủ đề nặng/cảm xúc)* |
| Highlight (tùy chọn) | `#F5E9D4` *(warm cream)*                                   |

### Thumbnail Composition Rules *(changed 25/09/2026 — overrides the colour table and font notes above)*

- **Plain white background**, no room, no scenery — except the "People who" group, where a faint everyday place behind Fred fades into the white.
- **Three headline groups (27/09/2026), 3 variants each, every /thumbnail run:** **A — PEOPLE WHO** (text stacked 3–4 lines, last word red), **B — LIST** (a big number first, the number highlighted in red, an honest count from the script), **C — CLASSIC** (2–5 word problem statement).
- **Title first, one thumbnail per title (29/09/2026):** `/metadata` writes 9 titles in the same A / B / C groups (A = "The Psychology of People Who…", B = "[N] Signs You…", C = "Why You…" or a warm reframe); `/thumbnail` then makes the thumbnail with the same id for each title. The headline adds what its title leaves out (feeling, gut-punch, cost) and never repeats it; a B pair shows the same number N.
- **Fred is the big main character** with an exaggerated, readable expression; 0–3 simple flat props / icons that show the problem.
- **Huge bold ALL-CAPS headline, generated inside the image** (the image model now spells reliably): 2–5 words naming the video's PROBLEM, black letters, **1–2 key words highlighted in red**. The exact colours live in the app's THUMBSTYLE field.
- **Layout:** headline across the top with Fred below, or Fred on one side with the headline stacked on the other — never text over Fred's face.
- **No pointing-hands motif as default.**
- **Consistency:** Fred always wears his plain white crew-neck T-shirt + dark indigo jeans + plain brown sneakers.
- **Contrast check:** at thumbnail size the headline must read in under a second.

## 7. CHANNEL STRATEGY

### Monetization roadmap

| Phase | Stage              | Focus                              | Revenue                                                                                                                                |
| ----- | ------------------ | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | 0–1k subs (Mo 1–3) | Nail the format + build watch time | Pre-monetization; build toward YPP                                                                                                     |
| **2** | 1k–10k subs        | YPP approved                       | AdSense + early channel memberships; affiliate (relevant books, guided journals)                                                       |
| **3** | 10k–50k subs       | Audience trust                     | Sponsorships (journaling / wellness apps), **digital product** (guided journal/workbook *"for people who notice everything"*), Patreon |
| **4** | 50k+ subs          | Brand                              | Own product line, courses, merch (signature-line apparel), licensing                                                                   |

> **YPP thresholds (US):** 1,000 subscribers **AND** 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months **OR** 10M valid public Shorts views in 90 days. A lower early-access tier (memberships/Super Thanks) also exists. *Verify current YouTube thresholds before planning — they change.*

### Content frequency

- **1 long-form video / week** — Sunday 7:00 PM ET (per DNA), ~12–19 min (script 2,500–4,000 words; the TTS reads ~215 wpm).
- Optional **1–2 Shorts / week** — repurpose each "sign" as a standalone Short (built-in series engine).
- Rotate the 5 content pillars to avoid format fatigue; lead with flagship topics.

### Topics nên TRÁNH

1. **No clinical diagnosis / treatment claims** — Fred is a friend, not a clinician. Relatable psychology, never medical advice.
2. **Crisis topics (suicide/self-harm) as hooks/listicles** — never sensationalize. If touched at all, handle with care and signpost professional help; never frame as a "sign" gimmick.
3. **Vague or overclaimed science** — keep the source generic ("researchers found…", "studies have linked…"; no names read out) and use a precise verb (found / measured / tracked / linked), stated at the scope the study measured; never "researchers suggest" or "studies point to".
4. **Trauma-dumping / graphic content** — keep it supportive and safe, not distressing.
5. **Pure overpromise clickbait** — protects long-term trust and the warm brand.
6. **Political / controversial tie-ins** — off-brand + demonetization risk.
