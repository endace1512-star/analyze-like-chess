STORYTELLING CRAFT (this is what raises a script a level — apply all three throughout, but NEVER at the cost of the guardrails above):

1. EMOTION = INNER MONOLOGUE, never a label. Do NOT announce a feeling ("I felt like a failure", "that day I was drained", "you feel so anxious"). Announcing a feeling only *reports* it — it does not make the viewer feel it with you. Instead, voice the actual thoughts running through the head in that second — the self-doubt, the spiral, the question on repeat — in first person (Fred's own) OR as the "you" version the viewer already thinks in their own head. The viewer recognizes themselves because they have thought those exact words.
   TELL (weak): "Walking in, I felt like a failure."
   SHOW: "Maybe I'm just not built for this, and if I freeze again that's it, because they already think I'm the weak one." One flowing line of real inner speech and the viewer is inside it — everyone has doubted themselves in exactly that voice. (Keep inner monologue flowing too — do not chop it into a string of short sentences.)

2. DROP INTO THE MOMENT — don't summarize the event. For any pivotal beat, do NOT report what happened ("they turned me down", "she went quiet on me"). Put the viewer in the room as it happens: one or two lines of the actual words spoken, ONE small physical beat (a phone slid into a pocket, a door clicking shut, a chair scraping back), then the ONE thought left hanging in the silence after. Present-tense immediacy over past-tense recap.
   SUMMARY (weak): "Another person pulled away from me."
   MOMENT: "She says let me think about it and get back to you, gives me a warm smile, and she's gone. I smile back and tell her of course, no rush, but the second the door clicks shut there's only one line left in my head, and it's here we go again." (This is also exactly what MODE SCENE draws downstream — writing the VO as a lived moment hands the visual pipeline a real beat to stage instead of a summary it can't picture.)

3. OPEN ON A SUBVERTED EXPECTATION (a true curiosity gap). The viewer arrives already predicting where this goes; confirm their prediction and they scroll. Plant instead ONE detail that runs AGAINST the obvious expectation so a "wait — why?" question fires, then pay it off honestly later. Use it in the hook, and to reopen attention right before a heavy sign.
   EXPECTED (skippable): "Here are five signs you notice too much."
   SUBVERTED: "The thing that drains you in a crowded room isn't the crowd — it's the one thing your mind does before anyone says a word." / "The part of you people call too sensitive is the same part that reads a room in three seconds."
   GUARDRAIL: this must be a GENUINE reframe grounded in the real mechanism — NOT clickbait. It never touches the banned "you won't believe / shocking / mind-blowing" register, and never promises a payoff the script does not actually deliver.

THE POINT (Fred's north star): good storytelling here is NOT inventing a dramatic story — it is choosing the true detail, the real feeling, and the exact moment, then telling them in the order that makes the viewer lean in. Pick the right detail over the loud one, every time.

THE CUT UNIT = THE CLAUSE (the picture changes at EVERY comma and EVERY full stop)
This overrides every rule below that uses the word "sentence" to decide WHERE to cut, HOW MANY units a row holds, HOW MANY pieces a row needs, or WHICH piece pairs with which words — read CLAUSE there. (TTS-safe punctuation cleaning, and "a sentence ends at . ! ?", are unchanged.) Build the unit list like this:

(2) MERGE SHORT: a clause under 4 words ("So,", "Well,", "of course,", "and honestly,", "Jaw tight,") is glued onto the NEXT clause of the same sentence; if it is the sentence's last clause, glue it onto the previous one. After merging, every clause has 4+ words (except rule 4).
(3) TECHNICAL COMMAS never cut: a comma inside a number (1,000).
(4) SIGNATURE LINES are fixed: "You're not broken. You're just paying attention." = TWO rows, both taken from the POSE LIBRARY (no generation): `[FN: pose:not-broken]` then `[FN: pose:paying-attention]`; "I know. Because I do it too." = ONE row, `[FN: pose:come-closer]`.
The app times each piece with the SAME rule (4-word merge, same number rule), so the counts must match it exactly.
How each mode carries one clause:
- MODE SCENE: ONE VISIBLE CHANGE = ONE row = ONE new image (same background name, same CONTINUITY STATE). A clause earns its own new image ONLY when it brings something the viewer can SEE change: a new action, a clearly new pose or expression, a prop entering / leaving / changing, a new shot (POV, a reaction close-up), a new place. HOLD MERGE (added 25/09/2026 — the editor was deleting ~1 in 4 scene images because runs of near-identical frames flashed by): a clause of the SAME sentence that only continues, qualifies, times or comments on the moment already on screen ("which should take three minutes", "at least not in the way I'd been telling myself", "and I know how that sounds", "one shoe in my hand", "looking perfectly calm from the outside") adds NO new image — it MERGES onto the previous row, which is then a HOLD row of 2-3 clauses of one sentence on one image. Never write 3+ consecutive scene rows whose actions differ only by a word of expression — that is the fault to kill; merge them into HOLD rows and save the new images for real changes. Other exception: an ENUMERATION carried by SCENE PIECES (case A below) — ONE clause = ONE piece revealed on the same scene image, up to 4 clauses and 2 sentences.
- MODE CONCEPT `[FN: present]` / `[FN: list]`: ONE clause = ONE new PIECE revealed on the same image (EXCEPT a NARRATION clause, which gets none — see below). A `present` row holds up to 4 clauses (4 pieces) and at most 2 sentences; the 5th clause or the 3rd sentence starts a new row. A LIST BEAT may hold up to 4 short parallel items (sentences or clauses), one piece each.
- `[FN: undergo]` / `[FN: demonstrate]` / `[FN: solo]` / `[FN: metaphor]` / `[FN: pose:…]`: nothing to reveal, so ONE clause = ONE row.
- A HUB row (PIECE DESIGN P5) is a `[FN: list]` row: the hub + up to 4 tiles, up to 2 sentences, each tile timed by its own `@word` anchor.
- NARRATION CLAUSES GET NO PIECE (added 28/09/2026 — pieces like "a stack of library books" for "I finally looked it up" or "a notebook titled MY THEORY" for "that was my theory about myself" told the viewer nothing and were wasted generations). A NARRATION clause is Fred narrating his own path to the idea rather than the idea itself: "so I looked it up", "when I finally looked into it", "turns out", "for years that was my theory about myself", "I used to think", "I remember", "here's what I found", "and here's the thing", plus any clause that is only a spoken filler. Such a clause gets NO piece and NO `{cell}`: Fred's pose and expression carry it. (1) On a `present` / `list` row it stays in the row's `vo` (it still merges and cuts as THE CUT UNIT says) and simply has no piece — the row's pieces are drawn only for its CONTENT clauses, each timed by its own `@word` anchor, so pieces are FEWER than clauses there. (2) Only the framing is dropped, never the content it carries: in "For years that was my theory about myself, that I came with faulty wiring," the "my theory" clause gets nothing and "faulty wiring" still gets its piece; in "Then I finally looked it up, and psychologists have a name for how we bring big feelings back down" only "looked it up" loses its piece. (3) A row whose `vo` is ONLY narration (e.g. "So I finally looked it up.") is a BRIDGE beat — `[FN: pose:reading]` for a search / research / citing-a-source line, `[FN: pose:thinking]` for an old belief — reusing a library image instead of a new generation. A row that MIXES narration with content ("Then I finally looked it up, and psychologists have a name for...") keeps its content pieces and takes its background from the library: `[FN: present pose:reading]` (see LIBRARY BASE FOR PIECE ROWS). (4) On a MODE SCENE row a narration clause never earns a case-B piece.
- A row that ends mid-sentence keeps the clause's own trailing comma in its `vo` ("The same four apps on a loop,"); the next row starts with the next word.
Expect roughly 1.5-2x more rows than a sentence-based sheet — more than that usually means HOLD clauses were cut into near-identical images.

CONTINUITY STATE (MODE SCENE — keeps Fred and the story consistent across cuts)
Every image is generated ALONE: the image tool sees only THIS row's action, never the row before, so anything that must persist has to be re-written on every row — otherwise the phone in Fred's hand, or the couch he sits on, silently disappears between cuts.
- A SEQUENCE = consecutive MODE SCENE rows in the same background. A RECOGNITION beat returning to MOMENT's background reuses that sequence's last STATE.
- At the start of each sequence, write ONE STATE phrase that LOCKS THE SET, 10-28 words, NO comma inside it: (1) Fred's exact body layout — sitting / lying / standing, which way he faces, and for lying WHERE his head and feet are ("lying on his back along the couch with his head on the left armrest"); (2) the anchor furniture named the SAME way every row ("the teal three-seat couch"); (3) what EACH hand holds (name the hand); (4) EVERY story prop the sequence's lines talk about, with its fixed place ("his open laptop on the coffee table in front of him"). A prop the story is ABOUT (the laptop full of deadlines, the phone that buzzes) stays in the STATE of every row until a clause removes it — otherwise it silently vanishes from later images; (5) NIGHT ROOM LIGHT (01/10/2026, replaces the 29/09 "one lamp switched on" rule — a named lamp made the model paint a beam / pool of light falling from it): a night-time row writes NO lighting at all in the Action — no lamp "switched on", no "under the lamp", no light, glow or shadow words. Night light comes ONLY from the time column (night-time) and the profile's NIGHT LIGHT cell, which lights the whole room evenly with ordinary room lighting. A lamp may still appear as plain furniture or a place ("beside the lamp on the nightstand"). E.g. "lying on his back along the teal couch with his head on the left armrest and his open laptop on the coffee table in front of him".
- CAMERA LOCK (added 25/09/2026): every row of a sequence uses the SAME camera value (default `medium shot`) seen from the same side, so the room and the furniture are redrawn the same way. THOUGHT / VOICE CLOSE-UP (added 30/09/2026, operator's rule — overrides the limits below): every row whose clause carries a THOUGHT or a VOICE uses camera `close-up` — Fred's inner voice or self-talk ("your head goes, like,", "why did you do that, she actually asked", "he'll say yes but he'll hate it"), a silent self-judgement, AND any words someone says out loud (every SPEECH BUBBLE row, case C), including the clause that introduces the line ("and she goes,", "your head goes,"). Frame the head and shoulders of whoever thinks or speaks, with the STATE's seat / wall / table edge just visible behind; in a two-person exchange the speaker's face fills the close-up and the listener may be cut at the frame edge (the Character column still lists both). A run of thought / voice rows is a run of close-ups — the 1-in-3 and never-two-in-a-row limits do not apply to them. Outside these rows a `close-up` is allowed only on a REACTION row whose line is about a bodily feeling, at most 1 row in 3, never on two such rows in a row; a POV row (SCENE PIECES case D) keeps the sequence camera — its over-the-shoulder look is the `{pov}` piece, not the camera. Apart from thought / voice rows, never alternate close-up / medium row by row — every framing switch re-invents the couch.
- EVERY row of the sequence starts its action with `[fred] ` + that STATE phrase WORD FOR WORD, then a comma, then ONLY what changes on this row (expression, gaze, head turn, the free hand). The change must fit the state: sitting with the phone he can look up, turn to camera, tighten his jaw — he cannot grip the couch arm with the phone hand, and he does not stand.
- NO-CLUTTER LOCK (added 01/10/2026, operator's rule): a row names ONLY the props its own clause or the STATE needs to tell the story — no decorative extras (a water bottle, a coffee cup, a bag, a plant, papers) added to make the shot feel lived-in. If a prop is not in the voice-over and not in the STATE, it is not in the action. /qcscene flags any action prop that no clause of its sequence talks about.
- The STATE changes ONLY when a clause makes it change (he puts the phone down, stands up, walks to the door). That row shows the change; the NEW STATE phrase is used from the next row on. A prop never vanishes, and never appears, without a clause causing it.
- A NAMED actor in the sequence is part of the STATE ("sitting on the couch holding his phone in his right hand while [modern-friend] sits beside him holding a mug"). Silhouette crowds are not.
- WHO-DOES-WHAT LOCK (added 27/09/2026 — the image model gives a bare "his / he" to the NEAREST name, so after "...while [modern-partner] leans in the doorway, one sneaker held in his right hand" the PARTNER got the sneaker, and "seen from behind over his shoulder" became the partner's shoulder): whenever a row names a second character, (1) the change part after the STATE restarts with a bracketed name — "[fred] holds one of his own sneakers...", "[fred] lowers his eyes while [modern-partner] throws both arms up" — never a bare verb, "his ..." or "he ..." straight after another character's name; (2) a `{pov}` piece is always Fred's (the app takes the first name of the Character column), so never write a POV for the other character — show their screen as a plain `{cell N}` piece instead; (3) every pronoun for a named actor matches that actor's SUBJECT gender (a man's SUBJECT -> his / him, never "her desk"); (4) the same character token never appears twice in one action — a metaphor with two copies of Fred writes "[fred] appears twice: on the left he..., on the right the same man..." (a second [fred] token gets printed on the image as a label).
Example (one sequence, three clause rows, all `medium shot`):
  `[fred] sitting deep in the teal couch holding his phone in his right hand with his closed laptop on the coffee table in front of him, thumb flicking the screen, jaw tight`
  `[fred] sitting deep in the teal couch holding his phone in his right hand with his closed laptop on the coffee table in front of him, eyes sliding to the laptop, flat face`
  `[fred] sitting deep in the teal couch holding his phone in his right hand with his closed laptop on the coffee table in front of him, turning toward camera, weary knowing face`

SCENE PIECES (MODE SCENE element layers — added 24/09/2026)
A lived image cannot carry everything the VO names: a list of worries, the exact words of a text, the time on a clock, an object too small to read. RULE: whatever the viewer needs to SEE but ONE scene image cannot clearly hold becomes a SCENE PIECE — a separate element image (one object on white, cut out by the app) that slides in over the scene image as its clause is spoken, exactly like a `present` piece on fred-white. Two cases:
- (A) ENUMERATION inside a lived moment ("the meeting at ten, the report I hadn't started, the call I'd been dodging for a week"): the enumerated clauses MERGE onto ONE scene row — ONE background image (Fred's STATE + one reaction), ONE piece per clause in speaking order, up to 4 clauses and at most 2 sentences. This is the only way a MODE SCENE row holds more than one clause. The clause that INTRODUCES the list ("Meanwhile my head was running the entire day at once,") and the clause after it stay their own rows.
- (B) ONE clause whose key detail the scene image cannot show legibly (a clock at 2 a.m. drawn with its hands, twenty minutes passing on a stopwatch, a ringing phone with vibration marks, a tiny object — all DRAWN without words, see TEXT IN IMAGE): the row keeps its ONE clause and adds ONE piece (two at most, the extra one named last) carrying that detail. Use it only when the detail matters to the line — never decorate a clause that the scene already tells. Words on a screen Fred is HOLDING and reading / typing are NOT a case-B piece: they go in a POV PIECE (case D).
- (D) POV PIECE (MANDATORY — added 02/10/2026, replaces the old full-frame POV row): a clause that states the WORDS Fred is typing, reading, deleting or receiving on a phone / screen / page he holds ("type sorry, I can't make it", "so you add so sorry", "delete the smiley face", "the email says see me") keeps its own scene row whose image is Fred in his STATE looking down at the device (face visible, normal sequence camera, expression allowed), and adds ONE POV piece closed by `{pov @word}` instead of `{cell N}`. The app draws that piece as Fred seen from behind, cropped at the shoulders, holding the device up with the screen readable, on white (POV STYLE cell), cuts it out and slides it UP from the bottom edge over the scene image as the anchor word is spoken. Write the POV piece as ONLY the screen content, NO comma inside, NO mention of Fred's head, shoulder, hand or framing (POV STYLE holds all of that): `his phone screen showing a chat app whose message box reads "<words>"`; the quoted words are the typed / read words up to ~8, spelled as spoken but WITHOUT commas ("sorry I can't make it"); an emoji is written in words ("a small smiley face emoji"); a deleted message = `his phone screen showing an empty chat message box`; a letter / laptop = `the letter in his hands reading "<words>"`. A POV row carries ONLY the POV piece (no `{cell N}` pieces) and at most one `{pov}`. In a typing / rewriting run, every clause that changes the words on the screen is its own row with its own POV piece, and the clauses about how Fred feels ("and then you just stare at it", "that feels too cold") stay plain REACTION rows between them.
- (C) SPEECH BUBBLE (MANDATORY — added 25/09/2026, narrowed the same day): a clause that gives words a character SAYS OUT LOUD to someone in the scene — a line of dialogue, two characters talking, Fred answering a friend, a boss snapping at him — carries ONE SPEECH BUBBLE piece (rounded outline with a pointed tail). THOUGHTS GET NO BUBBLE: inner monologue, the voice in his head, a silent self-judgement ("I'll do it later when I know what to say", "okay, the bill first", "what if one of them is urgent") are carried by Fred's pose and expression only — never a thought bubble, never a speech bubble. The clause that only INTRODUCES the line ("and she says,") gets no bubble; the clause with the spoken words does. BUBBLE TEXT = a SHORT CONDENSED version of the spoken words, not a verbatim quote: keep the full meaning in as few words as possible, about 2-6 words, in the speaker's own voice ("sorry, I really can't make it this Saturday" -> "Can't make Saturday. Sorry!"). No comma inside the text (a comma inside a piece breaks the app's piece reader) — use a period or ! instead. Write the piece as `a white speech bubble with a pointed tail at its lower left containing the words "<short text>" in a simple rounded cartoon font` (tail at its lower right when the bubble sits left of the speaker; the tail always points toward the SPEAKER's head — Fred or the named actor who talks). Put the bubble in a TOP cell beside the speaker's head (never `{cell 5}`, never covering a face). A bubble row still keeps Fred's STATE + change as usual; the bubble counts as that row's case-B piece. In a back-and-forth, each spoken clause is its own row with its own bubble.

  `[fred] <STATE phrase word for word>, <the row's change>, <piece 1> {cell N @word}, <piece 2> {cell N @word}` (the `@word` anchor as in ENCODING (b0))
  (a) NO `[FN: …]` flag on a scene row — the action still opens with `[fred] ` + the STATE phrase. The pieces come LAST, after Fred's change, each closed by `{cell N}`.
  (b) Pick cells that do NOT cover Fred or the prop his STATE names (Fred sitting on the right of the frame -> pieces in 1, 4, 7, 2). Top cells 1 / 2 / 3 are usually wall or sky and are the safest. Never `{cell 5}`.
  (c) Each piece = a standalone noun phrase with NO internal comma and no reference to Fred, drawn simply ("a phone showing a missed call"). A scene piece carries NO text except a speech bubble's condensed spoken words (see TEXT IN IMAGE): "a calendar card with 10 o'clock circled", not "a calendar card reading "10:00 MEETING"".
  (d) COUNT: case A -> pieces = clauses; case B -> one clause, 1-2 pieces; case C -> one clause, the bubble (+ at most one detail piece named after it); case D -> one clause, exactly one `{pov}` piece and nothing else.

ROSTER

- fred = the PROTAGONIST and ONLY recurring character (= character1, existing ref). Fred is on screen in EVERY scene — living the situation (MODE SCENE) or explaining on white (MODE CONCEPT). There is NO "you" character.
- supporting modern actors (modern-coworker, modern-boss, modern-friend, modern-partner, modern-mother, modern-father, modern-stranger, modern-child,...) = a SPECIFIC NAMED person the scene needs; they interact WITH Fred. Lowercase-hyphenated. NO ref sheet unless recurring more than 3 scenes episodes.
- generic crowds / unnamed groups (strangers on a street, a group entering a room, party guests, the office behind Fred, "someone else" as an abstract other in a CONCEPT metaphor) = NOT a named actor and NOT a roster entry; get NO SUBJECT. Do NOT list them in the Character column. Describe them ONLY inside the action as SHARP solid silver-grey silhouettes (hard black outline, no facial detail, fully clear — grey is a colour, not a fade) while Fred stays full-colour.

CLASSIFICATION — ask per unit, in THIS order:
(1) Does the line EXPLAIN an abstract concept / mechanism / term, OR is Fred addressing the viewer directly (a thesis, a pure bridge, a sign-title, the sign-off)? -> MODE CONCEPT (Fred alone on fred-white, concept imagery around him).
(2) Otherwise (a concrete situation / moment / feeling lived in context, or Fred inside a place-metaphor) -> MODE SCENE (Fred lives it in an everyday location).
Tie-breakers:

- A unit that MIXES a lived moment AND an explanation -> SPLIT it into one SCENE (lived half) + one CONCEPT scene (concept half). Never cram both into one scene.
- A pure transition/bridge sentence between signs -> MODE CONCEPT, addressed to the viewer, and it gets its OWN one-sentence BRIDGE beat drawn from the POSE LIBRARY (see BRIDGE BEATS below) instead of being absorbed onto a neighbour. Give it a SOLO-TO-CAMERA beat instead ONLY when it is a substantial direct-address that opens a real teaching stretch.
- Genuinely ambiguous -> default MODE SCENE. The channel's value is FEELING it; do not default to talking-head.

STAGING LAWS — by FUNCTION (contact must be load-bearing)
Fred is ALWAYS fully on-model and never opened / split / x-rayed / dissected / pierced (no object INSIDE his body or head). ONE Fred pose + expression per beat. Pick the FUNCTION that matches what the sentence is DOING, then write the action to match:

- PRESENT (the sentence EXPLAINS a concept / mechanism / term, or is a thesis / bridge / sign-title / sign-off): Fred stands in the centre — gesturing, or holding a NEUTRAL presenter prop (pointer stick, clipboard); by default his hands are empty (PIECE DESIGN P7) — while the concept pieces float SEPARATELY, each in its own zone. Fred does NOT hold or touch the concept object he is explaining. This is the default and the one that renders cleanly.
- DEMONSTRATE (the sentence tells the viewer an ACTION to take, or shows a change): Fred performs ONE clear action on / with a metaphor object (cutting a cord with scissors, flipping a switch off). Contact is fine here — the action IS the message.
- UNDERGO (the sentence shows something happening TO the viewer / affecting them): objects act on Fred — press, bind, pull toward him, weigh on him — and his expression carries it. He stays on-model (no rubber-stretched limbs, nothing fused into his face). Use silver-grey silhouettes for an abstract "someone else" doing it to him. UNDERGO renders as ONE whole image with the objects drawn in it (no element layers, nothing slides in), exactly like DEMONSTRATE — so an UNDERGO beat carries ONE sentence only.
- LIVE (= MODE SCENE): Fred LIVES the moment in an everyday location, grounded by ONE physical anchor at human scale (leaning on the counter, hand on the doorframe); his pose TRANSLATES the line WITHIN the sequence's CONTINUITY STATE (the anchor and the props in his hands carry over from row to row; only expression / gaze / free hand change); any NAMED other person interacts WITH Fred. NO "Fred watching from a distance" when the line is something Fred lives. Keep it simple (Fred + at most one named actor; generic crowds = silhouettes, no SUBJECT).

PIECES (MODE CONCEPT): draw concept objects DIRECTLY and simply — a warm-bordered message bubble with steam rising, an arrow, a frost-covered bubble — not over-clever visual riddles. Keep them full-colour and full-weight with the same bold outline as Fred, and ALWAYS SHARP — solid flat fills, hard black outline, fully clear. Grey (for the person-icons below) is a COLOUR that keeps Fred the focus, NOT a fade: nothing is ever faded, dimmed, blurred, translucent, or soft-edged. Max ~4 pieces per beat (a HUB row 5), each a SEPARATE free-floating piece in its own zone, none overlapping Fred or each other. NEVER box several sub-images into one divided panel/frame — the ONLY divided panel allowed is the one-clause SPLIT piece of PIECE DESIGN P4. EDITABILITY: draw every piece as a FLAT, hard-outlined object with flat fills — NEVER glowing / glow / aura / halo / bloom / light-rays / soft or feathered shadow (fuzzy edges can't be masked in CapCut); translate any "glowing X" into a plain outlined X (a "glowing red alarm light" -> "a flat red alarm-lamp with a bold black outline"). Hold each piece clearly AWAY from Fred, in its own zone, so it cuts cleanly. A concept that is a PERSON (a friend who went quiet, a stressed coworker, an elite violinist, an enumerated "they were walking, busy, texting") becomes a PIECE DESIGN P1 VIGNETTE: a small simple full-colour cartoon extra (never Fred, never realistic) doing the action with 1-2 props, no room around it. (Changed 25/09/2026 — the old silver-grey silhouette icon is retired for pieces; silhouettes remain only for unnamed crowds inside MODE SCENE images.)
PIECE DESIGN (added 25/09/2026, modelled on the big psychology explainer channels — OVERRIDES any older line in this file asking for "a clean icon", a silhouette person-icon, or banning a divided panel, wherever they conflict). A piece is a small illustrated STORY the viewer reads in one second, not a lone clip-art icon. Pick the type that fits the clause; one row may mix types. Every piece stays ONE noun phrase with NO internal comma — join its parts with "with / and / beside / next to".
(P1) VIGNETTE (the default) — a tiny self-contained scene: one small simple cartoon person doing the clause's action plus 1-2 props that make it unmistakable ("a small cartoon man asleep in a bed beside a wall clock", "a small cartoon woman playing a violin next to an almost full green progress bar"). The person is a generic modern everyday extra in the channel's cartoon style, full colour, NEVER Fred, NEVER realistic, never given a room or floor around it — only the props named. A plain object is still a valid piece when the clause names just an object.
(P2) DATA — every number, amount, duration, frequency, percentage, ranking or comparison the VO states becomes a visual that SHOWS the size: a bar chart (the winning bar taller and green), a small two-column table of 3-5 short rows, a gauge with its needle, a battery at the stated level, a clock face, a progress bar, a calendar with marked days. NOTHING is written on it (changed 30/09/2026) — not even a research finding's number; the VO says the number and the drawing shows the size ("a bar chart with blank unlabeled axes and one tall green bar beside a short blue bar"); an everyday time, duration or count is SHOWN, not written (clock hands, a half-full progress bar, eleven drawn ice cubes) — see TEXT IN IMAGE. Pair it with a P1 person when someone does the thing ("a small cartoon man napping on a couch next to a battery charging to full").
(P3) CAPTION — RETIRED 30/09/2026: no piece carries a caption any more (TEXT IN IMAGE); the rest of this paragraph is history only. (Old rule: the key piece of a row may carry a short HANDWRITTEN caption beside it with a small curved arrow pointing at the part that matters: `with a handwritten caption "more rest" and a small curved arrow pointing at the tall bar`. Caption = 1-4 lowercase words, used ONLY for a research finding's number or a named concept (never a verdict or a paraphrase of the VO such as "more to catch" or "feels everything"). RARE: at most 1 captioned piece per row, and most rows have none. A NAMED-CONCEPT label may be written as this caption instead of a tag.)
(P4) CONTRAST — a line comparing two states (average vs top performer, fake vs real rest, before vs after) is a MIRRORED PAIR: the SAME drawing twice with only the telling detail changed (short blue bar vs tall green bar, empty vs full battery, a tired face vs a calm face). Two clauses -> TWO pieces opposite each other ({cell 4} and {cell 6}, or {cell 1} and {cell 3}) in speaking order. One clause stating both sides -> ONE SPLIT piece: `a split panel with a vertical divider line showing <left state> on the left and <right state> on the right` ("a split panel with a vertical divider line showing a tired cartoon man buried in scattered sticky notes beside an empty battery on the left and the same man calm beside neatly stacked colour blocks on the right").
(P5) HUB — a line that introduces a SET (the four mechanisms, three steps, a toolkit) and names its items is a hub board on a `[FN: list]` row: the hub and its tiles are grouped by PLACEMENT only — NO connector lines, spokes, arrows or stubs on any tile (changed 28/09/2026: each tile is cut out alone, so a line drawn on it just dangles in mid-air). Piece 1 = the HUB, one container object that carries the set's theme ("an open grey toolbox holding a violin and a practice sheet"), in {cell 2}, anchored on the word that names the set. Each item after it = one TILE: `a <pastel colour> rounded square tile with a <single icon>` — NO label on the tile (changed 30/09/2026, TEXT IN IMAGE; the VO names each item as its tile slides in) and nothing attached to its edges, placed around the hub in {cell 1}, {cell 3}, {cell 4}, {cell 6} in speaking order, each anchored on the item's own word. A HUB row holds the hub + up to 4 tiles (5 pieces) and up to 2 sentences. When the items are single words that THE CUT UNIT merges into one clause ("detachment, relaxation, mastery, and control"), the anchors still time each tile to its word — so on a HUB row pieces may outnumber clauses.
(P6) FULL FRAME — Fred stays in the centre (the editor moves him by hand when a frame needs it). The pieces FILL the free space around him like a busy explainer board, big and close together, not a few small objects adrift in white: fill the top row 1-2-3 first, then 4 and 6, then 7 and 9. With 3+ pieces the camera is `wide establishing shot`.
(P7) EMPTY HANDS BY DEFAULT — on `present` / `list` / `solo` rows Fred holds NOTHING: his pose and expression carry the feeling (open arms, a raised finger, hands on hips). A small emotion prop inside his pose (two yellow sparkles in his raised hands, a magnifying glass at his eye) is a rare exception, only when the line truly cannot read without it — never routine, never a piece, never with a `{cell}`, never the concept object he is explaining.
(P8) NO STOCK METAPHORS (added 30/09/2026 — one episode drew "burden" as the same heavy backpack 6 times, "feelings" as cardboard boxes 5 times, plus an iceberg, a storm cloud, a scale and a megaphone): when a clause uses a figure of speech or an abstract word, do NOT draw the word's literal stock symbol. BANNED stock images: backpack / sack / boulder / rock / pebble / weight for "burden, heavy, too much, a little"; stack of boxes for "feelings, problems, carrying"; iceberg for "underneath, hidden"; storm / rain cloud for "stress, sadness, what's going on"; balance scale / seesaw for "fairness, owing, even"; megaphone / loudspeaker for "voice, loud"; lightbulb for "idea"; chains / ball-and-chain for "stuck"; battery for "energy, tired" (except as a P2 DATA gauge of a stated level); broken heart for "hurt, breakup"; mask for "hiding, pretending". Instead draw what the phrase MEANS in a person's life as a P1 VIGNETTE — a small cartoon person doing the telling action or showing the telling reaction: "you'd be a burden" -> "a small cartoon man hesitating at a friend's door with his hand raised to knock and pulled back"; "handing someone a burden" -> "a small cartoon man staring at his phone with his thumb frozen over the send button"; "holding everyone else's feelings" -> "a small cartoon man hugging three crying friends at once with both arms full"; "what's going on underneath" -> "a small cartoon man smiling brightly while his shadow on the wall behind him slumps with its head down"; "whatever you're carrying" -> "a small cartoon man sitting down beside a friend with his shoulders finally dropping". NO REPEATS: within one video a metaphor image is never reused — when the same idea comes back (burden in the hook, the sign title, the mechanism and the Lift), each return gets a different vignette.

VISUAL CONTINUITY WITHIN A SIGN (cohesion, not a straitjacket)
Prefer DIRECT, simple depiction of what each sentence means over clever metaphor riddles. For cohesion, REUSE a recurring visual element within a sign where it fits naturally (a message bubble that keeps returning, a red alarm light, a silhouette other-person) so the sign reads as one visual story — but you are NOT required to force one single metaphor object through every scene, and different beats inside a sign will genuinely show different pictures. Do not manufacture a strained metaphor just to keep one object on screen; if the plainest reading is a warm bubble turning cold, draw that.

THE FIXED SIGN TEMPLATE (every one of the N signs; N = 5 by default, or the number in a Formula C "[N] Signs..." title)
0. SIGN-TITLE (MODE CONCEPT, Fred SOLO on fred-white) — every sign OPENS with a Fred-solo scene on fred-white: character = fred, PRESENT function (Fred to one side gesturing toward the sign's idea drawn as ONE clear floating element per announcing sentence he does NOT touch), camera per piece-density (usually medium). The vo is the sign's announcing/thesis sentence (the line stating what this sign is); if the script has no distinct announcing line, split the first sentence so the clause stating the sign's idea becomes this scene. This beat OBEYS the ≤2 cap: it holds at most 2 announcing sentences (with the [SIGN-TITLE] tag on it). Any FURTHER thesis sentences do NOT go into the sign-title beat and do NOT wait for the sign's MECHANISM: they form an EXTRA fred-white CONCEPT beat inserted immediately AFTER the sign-title and BEFORE the MOMENT scene (same background, a new Fred pose, its own pieces, no [SIGN-TITLE] tag — only the first beat of a sign carries that tag). HOOK / ROOT / LIFT

- HOOK: SCENE (Fred at the doorway, already drained, living it) -> CONCEPT (Fred solo on white, promise-loop to the viewer).
- ROOT: CONCEPT (Fred teaches the origin + the core concept on white) interleaved with SCENE (Fred living the earlier-self moment) -> close on the signature line = CONCEPT (Fred to camera on white).
- LIFT: SCENE (deepest moment, Fred living it) -> CONCEPT or SCENE (the lightening) -> "one small shift" -> sign-off = CONCEPT (Fred to camera on white).

SHOT VOCABULARY (internal rubric — ONE IMAGE = ONE MEANING)
The deepest cut rule is MEANING: each beat shows the ONE thing the current sentence needs on screen. Read each sentence's FUNCTION, pick the SHOT that delivers it, and CUT A NEW BEAT whenever the required shot changes. These shot names are an INTERNAL rubric — they are NEVER written into any column; they only drive WHERE you cut and WHAT the action says. /qcscene re-derives them to check fit.

MODE SCENE shots (Fred living it, real background):

- ENV — establish Fred in a real place with a human-scale anchor; the base "living the moment" shot.
- POV / INSERT — the EXACT trigger object Fred reads / receives / types (a phone screen reading "Sounds good.", a stern email). Use when the sentence points at a specific thing being read/received. Since 02/10/2026 it is NEVER a full-frame shot: it is a POV PIECE `{pov @word}` on the scene row (SCENE PIECES case D).
  POV PIECE FRAMING (02/10/2026): the scene row shows Fred in his STATE looking down at the device, with the sequence camera (no `over-the-shoulder close-up`, face and expression allowed). The over-the-shoulder look — Fred from behind, cropped at the shoulders, device held up with the screen readable, white background — lives in the profile's POV STYLE cell, so the POV piece text names ONLY the screen content. Never describe the room Fred faces.
  MANDATORY (added 25/09/2026, re-encoded 02/10/2026): a clause that states the WORDS Fred is typing, reading, deleting or receiving on a screen or page he holds MUST carry a POV piece — never a plain reaction shot with the words left out, and never the words drawn into the scene image. Encoding and examples: SCENE PIECES case D.
- REACTION — tight on Fred's body/face carrying a bodily/inner response (chest dropping, shoulders climbing, jaw tight). Use when the sentence names a feeling/sensation.
- ESCALATION — SAME place & subject, pose/expression RAMPED to raise the stakes (gripping the phone, leaning in). A new beat because the expression changed.
- EXHALE — the release that closes a scene mini-arc: Fred loosening, setting the phone down, shoulders dropping as it turns out fine.
- THRESHOLD — Fred crossing into a space and FREEZING on a detected shift (through a doorway, catching a quiet person, stopped mid-step). Use for "your body alarms before your mind knows why".

MODE CONCEPT shots (Fred on fred-white; every object flat + hard-outlined + NO glow):

- SIGN-TITLE — the sign's opener: Fred solo + ONE revealable element per announcing sentence embodying the sign (a single-sentence title = ONE hero object; a 2-sentence title = one element per sentence, obeying the ≤2 cap) + the [SIGN-TITLE: ...] tag (see THE FIXED SIGN TEMPLATE).
- BRIDGE — a connective / pivot sentence that carries no new picture of its own ("Here's what's actually happening", "But that's not what's going on", "Nobody ever explains this", "Wait."). It gets its own ONE-sentence beat on fred-white showing a stock narrator pose from the POSE LIBRARY, so the cut keeps moving instead of holding the neighbouring image through the connective. NO pieces, NO reveal, and the image is NOT generated — the app reuses a library file (see BRIDGE BEATS AND THE POSE LIBRARY).
- SOLO-TO-CAMERA — Fred alone facing camera, NO concept objects, talking straight to the viewer (thesis, empathy line, "you're not broken", sign-off). Use for a SUBSTANTIAL direct address that stands on its own. EXCEPTION — a SHORT re-anchor line dropped inside a lived moment ("I know.", "I do it too.", "I've done it too.") does NOT get pulled onto fred-white: it STAYS in the surrounding MODE SCENE beat as its own one-sentence row in that scene's background, with Fred turning toward camera from inside the room. Pulling a two-word re-anchor onto the white void breaks the lived moment and is a MODE flag; only move it to fred-white when it opens a real teaching or thesis stretch.
- PIECES — Fred presenting SEVERAL separate pieces in their own zones, filling the frame around him (the list / preview beat), each drawn per PIECE DESIGN (vignette / data / caption / hub tile). A "person" item = a small full-colour cartoon extra (P1), never Fred, never realistic.
- NEGATION — Fred rejecting labels: a disagreement pose (wagging index finger, flat "stop" palm, arms crossed) with the rejected labels as separate bubbles around him.
- METAPHOR — ONE image that ENACTS the mechanism the sentence describes, BOUND TO the sentence's core keyword. Name the keyword, then stage the exact behaviour the words state — e.g. "emotional hypervigilance ... locked into an alert state, scanning for relational not physical danger" -> a radar/alarm turned INWARD, its sweep passing over a small sharp physical-danger icon (set aside, not dimmed) and locking onto relational cues (a period, a cooler face, a silent bubble), a small lock holding it in the alert state. Not literal, not a sterile clinical diagram; the concept keyword must survive here (see ANCHOR PROTECTION).
  FULL-FRAME METAPHOR `[FN: metaphor]` (added 25/09/2026): when the line states THE big contrast, choice or cost of a section ("the best performers aren't the ones grinding non-stop, they're the ones who rest on purpose"), draw the WHOLE 16:9 frame as one illustrated world instead of pieces on white — a road forking into two paths, a mine and a meadow, a sinking ship. Fred may appear up to 3 TIMES, each copy playing a role in a costume that tells it ("[fred] in a hard hat trudging exhausted along a conveyor belt beside a grinding wheel on the left", "[fred] in a straw hat sipping juice in a hammock between two trees on the right"), plus at most ONE narrator copy observing ("[fred] at top centre peering through a magnifying glass"). Each copy has its own pose + expression. Supporting symbols (an empty red battery on the grind side, a full green battery on the rest side) are drawn in. ONE clause = ONE row, camera `wide establishing shot`, no element layers, no `@` anchors needed; each zone MAY end with `{cell N}` for staging (the app strips it). It is the most expensive image to generate: about 1-3 per video, only for the lines that carry a section's turn. The app draws it with the METAPHOR STYLE box.
- CONTRAST / SPLIT — two states side by side to show a difference ("sounds good!" warm vs "sounds good." flat; a bright group-chat vs one grey flat bubble; two bubbles drifting apart). Drawn as a PIECE DESIGN P4 mirrored pair (two clauses) or one split piece with a divider (one clause); a section's headline contrast may instead be a FULL-FRAME METAPHOR.
- TRANSFORM — a threat->warmth turn. The tool cannot animate, so a TRANSFORM is SPLIT INTO 2+ beats/rows: a BEFORE image (the threat motifs around Fred) and an AFTER image (the SAME motifs re-formed into something warm), the turn carried by visual RHYME between them. Keep every motif WELL CLEAR of Fred, each in its own zone, hard-outlined, so it masks / animates separately in CapCut.

CALLBACK: when a beat REUSES an earlier shot (RECOGNITION reusing MOMENT's background, or a repeated POV), RE-DESCRIBE that shot in this beat's action so a FRESH near-identical image is generated — do NOT point at the old row or reuse a file. Every row generates its own image (1:1).

PUNCTUATION — VO GOES STRAIGHT TO TTS
Every vo field may contain ONLY: period .  comma ,  question ?  exclamation !  (PLUS two kept forms: a contraction apostrophe — you're, don't, that's — and a hyphen INSIDE a compound word — tired-but-wired, five-second, almond-shaped; these stay as written and are NOT violations).
Convert / remove everything else while cleaning:

- em-dash —, en-dash –, a hyphen used AS A PAUSE (a word , dash , word aside)  ->  comma  (but KEEP a hyphen that joins one compound word)
- ellipsis … or ...  ->  comma
- semicolon ;, colon :  ->  comma
- straight/curly QUOTE marks " ', parentheses ( ), brackets [ ], asterisks *, slashes /  ->  delete  (but KEEP contraction apostrophes: you're, don't stay intact)
- all markdown (## ** * ---), [stage directions], (visual cues), checklists, metadata  ->  delete
  One space after every comma/period. When several sentences share one beat, join them with a single space and keep each sentence's own capitalization and terminal . ! or ? — do NOT fuse distinct sentences into one comma run.
  
  

HARD RULE — ONE BEAT = ONE ROW; ONE IMAGE = ONE BEAT
Every ROW is one BEAT = one generated image (see THE IMAGE UNIT below). A beat's `vo` holds EVERY sentence spoken while that one image is on screen, in order, joined into a single line. No sentence is ever dropped, summarized, or reordered; every sentence of the script survives inside exactly one beat's `vo`, and the beats run in script order. Forming a beat's `vo`: read the script sentence by sentence (a sentence ends at . ! or ?), clean each to TTS-safe punctuation, then APPEND it to the CURRENT beat's `vo` — starting a NEW beat/row the moment the image must change (see CUT A NEW BEAT). Join the sentences of one beat with a single space, each keeping its own capitalization and terminal . ! or ?. Do NOT merge two beats: the row boundary is the IMAGE boundary. In MODE CONCEPT a held moment can span at most 2 sentences on one image (or 3-4 in a LIST BEAT), carried by per-sentence reveals, so there the image boundary is wider than the sentence; in MODE SCENE the image boundary is the VISIBLE CHANGE — normally one clause per image, a HOLD clause (no visible change) merged onto the row before it, never two sentences on one image. (`vo` feeds only the app's SRT alignment, matched word-by-word case-insensitively, so its punctuation never reaches TTS — keep it clean for readability and reliable matching.)

THE IMAGE UNIT — one generated image = one BEAT = one ROW
The image tool is text->image ONLY (it cannot edit an existing image), so all the sentences spoken over one unchanging picture live together in ONE beat = ONE row = ONE generated image. Two tiers:

- BEAT = one generated image = one composition + one Fred pose/expression. Its `vo` carries every sentence spoken while that image is on screen.
- PIECE = one separate concept object in that image (a bubble, arrow, card, silhouette). On fred-white a beat holds at most ~4 pieces, each in its own clear zone, none overlapping Fred or each other. The beat's image is generated with ALL its pieces visible at once — there is no per-sentence un-masking in the scene sheet; any timed reveal is done by hand in CapCut on that single already-complete image.

CUT A NEW BEAT (new row, new generated image) whenever the VISUAL MOMENT genuinely changes: MODE (CONCEPT<->SCENE), BACKGROUND, Fred's POSE/EXPRESSION, or the SHOT the sentence needs (a phone POV, then a REACTION on Fred's face, then a SOLO-TO-CAMERA are THREE moments even in one place). Also cut when a CONCEPT beat already holds 2 sentences (LIST BEAT excepted — up to 4 parallel items) or ~4 pieces, and always SPLIT a TRANSFORM into before/after.

MODE DECIDES HOW A MOMENT IS CARRIED — this is the split rule (the single most important cut decision):
(Scope: the one-sentence-per-image split applies ONLY to everyday-location SCENE beats. Scenes on the protagonist's own concept background — fred-white — are EXEMPT: they KEEP the old multi-sentence-per-image behavior for easy editing, exactly as before. Never split a fred-white beat just to hit one-sentence-per-image.)

- MODE CONCEPT (bg = fred-white): a continuous moment KEEPS its sentences on ONE image. The white void has room for floating pieces, so give the image ONE separate on-screen ELEMENT per sentence / sub-point (≤~4, each in its own zone around Fred, hard-outlined so it masks cleanly), listed in speaking order; the editor masks them all and REVEALS them one at a time as the VO lands — motion from ONE consistent generation. Do NOT spin up a fresh generation per sentence here (the tool re-invents the void's objects each run and drifts).
  (CLAUSE UPDATE: a `present` / `list` row reveals ONE element per CLAUSE, up to 4 clauses and at most 2 sentences per image — see THE CUT UNIT. The ≤2 cap below therefore counts SENTENCES, while the piece count follows CLAUSES.)
  HARD CAP (≤2): a MODE CONCEPT beat holds AT MOST 2 sentences (one revealable element per clause) — NEVER 3-4 sentences of continuous teaching on one image. A CONCEPT stretch that would run 3+ sentences is SPLIT into ≤2-sentence beats (same background name; Fred's changing pose / new element is the cut). TWO exceptions to the cap, and ONLY these two:
  (1) LIST BEAT — 3-4 CONSECUTIVE, grammatically PARALLEL enumeration items, each naming ONE same-category thing that reads as ONE icon, all spoken under ONE unchanging Fred pose (shot = PIECES). It may hold up to 4 items on ONE image, one icon per item in speaking order (trend still ≤4 pieces). TEST: "could these lines be replaced by a short noun bullet-list (walking, busy, texting)?" YES -> LIST BEAT (stay merged, ≤4). If instead each line OPENS its own idea / mechanism needing explanation -> NOT a list -> obey the ≤2 cap and split. A LIST BEAT is a special beat-type like SIGN-TITLE / TRANSFORM.
  (2) SOLO-TO-CAMERA — Fred alone facing camera, NO concept objects (a thesis, empathy line — the signature lines themselves now come from the POSE LIBRARY, see THE CUT UNIT (4) — "you're not broken", sign-off). It carries no pieces by design, so it is EXEMPT from the reveal-per-sentence requirement (never a flat-beat), but it STILL obeys the ≤2 cap (a 3-sentence sign-off splits into two talking-head beats).
  ONE-IMAGE FUNCTIONS (`undergo` / `demonstrate`): the merge above rides on pieces being revealed one at a time, and only `present` / `list` rows get that — an `undergo` or `demonstrate` row is generated as ONE finished image with its objects already drawn in, so nothing can be revealed on it. Treat those two like MODE SCENE: ONE sentence = ONE beat = ONE image. A 2-sentence stretch that wants UNDERGO staging is split into two UNDERGO beats reusing the fred-white bg, Fred's changing pose carrying the cut.
- MODE SCENE (any everyday location, bg ≠ fred-white): ONE visible change = ONE beat = ONE image — normally one clause, with HOLD clauses merged (see THE CUT UNIT; every row of a same-place run carries the CONTINUITY STATE). Do NOT merge multiple sentences onto a single everyday image. A real location has NO floating pieces to reveal, so a multi-sentence SCENE image just sits static (a flat beat) — instead give each sentence its own image so every lived beat (the REACTION, the ESCALATION, the EXHALE, the thought left in the silence) actually lands on screen. When consecutive SCENE beats are the SAME location, REUSE the exact same background name so the room stays locked to its reference: the tool re-renders the place, but Fred's changing pose/expression is the reason for the cut and the locked bg name holds the location. (A single sentence may still split into 2 beats if its shot changes mid-sentence; it is never merged with its neighbour — EXCEPT an enumeration carried by SCENE PIECES, whose clauses merge onto one scene row with one piece each, and a HOLD clause, which merges onto the row before it.)

The unit is the MOMENT. In CONCEPT the moment can span several sentences (carried by reveals on one image) — but ONLY on a `present` / `list` row, because only those get per-piece layers; an `undergo` / `demonstrate` row is one finished image and takes ONE sentence, like SCENE. In SCENE the moment is ONE sentence (carried by Fred's changing pose across separate images). Two faults to kill: (1) OVER-MERGE — sentences that need DIFFERENT shots crammed onto one image (a phone POV + a face reaction + a direct address); split by shot. (2) FLAT BEAT — in CONCEPT, a multi-sentence image with nothing to reveal per sentence (one static element sitting 15+ seconds); in SCENE, ANY image holding 2+ sentences. Fix CONCEPT by naming one revealable element per sentence; fix SCENE by giving each sentence its own image. Never a flat frame.

ENCODING (no new columns — the app schema is unchanged):

- Each beat = ONE row. Its `action` = the FULL composition — Fred's single pose/expression + EVERY piece, the pieces listed in the exact order their sentences SPEAK them (speaking order, NOT spatial order — the app pairs piece 1 with sentence 1, piece 2 with sentence 2, and times each piece's on-screen entrance to its own sentence; a spatially-sorted list makes the wrong piece appear on the wrong line). This one row is generated once and produces exactly one image; there are no follow-on rows for it.
- ELEMENT ENCODING (MODE CONCEPT rows — machine-read by the app, which builds the CapCut draft; MODE SCENE rows use the SCENE PIECES shape instead: no flag, STATE first, pieces last with `{cell N}`). Two markers, both MANDATORY on every fred-white row, and the action is written in this fixed shape. WHICH ROWS ANIMATE: the app lifts pieces onto their own animated layers on `[FN: present]` and `[FN: list]` rows ONLY; `[FN: undergo]`, `[FN: demonstrate]` and `[FN: solo]` rows stay ONE whole generated image with everything drawn in it. Both markers are still written on every fred-white row regardless — the app reads the flag to decide, and strips both markers from the image prompt:
  `[FN: <function>] [fred] <one pose + one expression>, <piece 1> {cell N @word}, <piece 2> {cell N @word}` (+ ` [SIGN-TITLE: …]` last, on a sign-title row only)
  (a) START the action with the FUNCTION flag: `[FN: present]` (pieces float free, Fred does not touch them — the default), `[FN: list]` (a LIST BEAT, shot = PIECES), `[FN: undergo]` (objects act on Fred — ONE whole image, no layers, ONE sentence), `[FN: demonstrate]` (Fred acts on one object — ONE whole image, no layers, ONE sentence), `[FN: solo]` (SOLO-TO-CAMERA, no pieces at all), `[FN: metaphor]` (FULL-FRAME METAPHOR — the whole frame one drawn world, Fred up to 3 times in roles, ONE whole image, no layers, ONE clause; see SHOT VOCABULARY, METAPHOR), `[FN: pose:<pose-name>]` (BRIDGE beat — image reused from the POSE LIBRARY, no pieces, exactly one sentence; see BRIDGE BEATS AND THE POSE LIBRARY). SIGN-TITLE rows use `[FN: present]`. A row with pieces is NEVER `[FN: solo]` and NEVER `[FN: pose:…]`.
  (b0) ANCHOR (added 25/09/2026, MANDATORY on every piece of every row, concept AND scene): inside the cell tag, after the number, write `@` + the word(s) of THIS row's `vo` that the piece stands for, copied exactly as spoken, 1-3 words: `{cell 3 @amygdala}`, `{cell 1 @meeting at ten}`. The app slides the piece in the moment the voice says that word, so pick the word that NAMES the thing the piece shows (usually its noun), never a filler like "and" or the clause's first word. Two pieces of one row never share an anchor, and anchors follow speaking order.
  (b) END every piece with its grid cell `{cell N}` (with its anchor: `{cell N @word}`), replacing the old zone words — never write `(left)`, `(centre)`, `(right)`, `(above)` on a fred-white row. The 16:9 frame is a 3x3 grid numbered 1,2,3 (top row) / 4,5,6 (middle row) / 7,8,9 (bottom row). Fred stands at least 2 cells tall, so pieces take ONLY cells Fred does not occupy. Default recipe (use it unless the beat truly needs otherwise): 1 piece -> `{cell 3}`; 2 pieces -> `{cell 1}` then `{cell 3}`; 3 pieces -> `{cell 1}`, `{cell 2}`, `{cell 3}`; a LIST BEAT of 4 -> fill in the priority order 1 > 3 > 4 > 6 > 7 > 9. Use a middle cell (4 or 6) only when Fred is clearly on the opposite side, and NEVER `{cell 5}` (Fred stands there).
  (c) WRITE EACH PIECE as a clean standalone noun phrase with NO internal comma and no reference to Fred — on a `present` / `list` row the app also generates that piece on its own as a separate element image on empty white, so it must read without him ("a flat speedometer with its needle pinned into the red zone", not "a speedometer he points at, needle red"). EXCEPTION — `undergo` and `demonstrate` rows: those are drawn as one whole image, no piece is cut out on its own, so a piece there MAY and usually SHOULD name its relation to Fred ("a heavy grey slab pressing down on his shoulders"), since that contact is the whole point of the function. The no-internal-comma rule still holds on every row. Commas separate one piece from the next, nothing else. Fred's pose + expression come FIRST, before the first piece, never trailing after the last one.
  (d) COUNT: on a present row the number of pieces EQUALS the number of CLAUSES in that row's vo (after the 4-word merge of THE CUT UNIT) MINUS its NARRATION clauses (see THE CUT UNIT) — one revealable element per content clause, in speaking order (1 clause = 1 piece, 3 clauses = 3 pieces, 3 clauses of which one is "I finally looked it up" = 2 pieces, each with its `@word` anchor). A mismatch makes the app time pieces to the wrong words. On a LIST BEAT the enumeration items ARE the sentences, so its 3-4 pieces match its 3-4 items one-for-one. The "max ~4 pieces" line is a CEILING on how busy one frame may get, never a licence to out-number the sentences: a 2-sentence beat never carries 3 pieces. If a beat genuinely needs a small extra prop beyond its sentence count, name it LAST — the app times any surplus piece to the final sentence. EXCEPTION — a HUB row (PIECE DESIGN P5): hub + one tile per named item, each on its own `@word` anchor, so pieces may outnumber clauses there.
  present / list rows get one element image per piece; undergo / demonstrate / solo rows stay a single image (still write the `[FN: …]` flag, and on an undergo or demonstrate row still give every object a `{cell N}` — the app strips it, but it keeps the staging honest and keeps the row machine-checkable).
